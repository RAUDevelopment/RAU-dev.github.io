Frequently Asked Questions

Is this image to text tool really free?

Yes. The tool is free to use and supported by ads. There is no daily limit and no paid tier.

Do you save my uploaded images?

No. The text is read on your own device using JavaScript. The image is never uploaded, so there is nothing for us to save.

Can I extract text from handwritten notes?

Partly. It is built for printed text and clear screenshots. Messy handwriting will give you errors, so check anything important.

What file types and sizes work?

JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF and BMP, up to 15MB. Very large photos are scaled down first, which is faster and usually more accurate.

Why does the first run take longer?

The recognition engine downloads once the first time you use it. After that it is cached and runs straight away.

What is OCR?

OCR stands for optical character recognition. When you take a photo of a receipt, a page of a book or a slide from a lecture, what you get is a grid of coloured pixels. No computer can copy words out of that. OCR reads the shapes, matches them against letterforms it already knows, and rebuilds them as real, selectable text you can paste anywhere.

Why this one runs in your browser

Most free OCR sites upload your image to a server, read it there and send the text back. That means a stranger's machine holds a copy of your ID card, bank statement or medical letter. This tool downloads the recognition engine once and runs it on your own device. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored, and there is no account to cancel.

What it handles well

Printed text from screenshots, PDF exports, document photos, book pages and signs converts almost perfectly. Handwriting, low light photos and heavily stylised fonts are harder. Expect some errors there, and check anything you plan to rely on.

Limits worth knowing

Supported files are JPG, PNG, WEBP, GIF and BMP, up to 15MB. Images bigger than about 2000 pixels are scaled down before reading. Smaller images are faster and often more accurate, because the engine gets a cleaner look at each letter.
